# IVO Studio — Master Visual Design Specification

## 1. Brand concept

IVO Studio is a design identity built around precision, reduction, and spatial intelligence. Its visual language combines minimalist architectural branding with a premium editorial sensibility: strong black fields, a bold white geometric monogram, carefully controlled construction lines, and generous negative space.

The identity should feel:

- Architectural and structural
- Technical but not industrial
- Quiet, premium, and confident
- Experimental through geometry, not decoration
- Editorial, with disciplined typography and composition

The central principle is **clarity through construction**. Every shape, line, and gap should appear intentional.

## 2. Logo system

### Primary mark

The primary logo is a bold, custom geometric **IVO** monogram in white, normally placed on a black or near-black field. The mark should read clearly at a glance while retaining distinctive architectural character.

The monogram should use:

- Custom geometric letterforms rather than a standard font
- Strong verticals, horizontals, and controlled diagonals
- Consistent stroke logic and optical weight
- Deliberate cut-outs, joins, and counters
- A compact silhouette with a clear overall rhythm

### Secondary lockup

When the full studio name is required, pair the monogram with the wordmark **IVO STUDIO**. The wordmark should remain subordinate to the monogram and use refined, letterspaced typography.

Preferred arrangements:

1. Monogram above the wordmark for formal, centered compositions
2. Monogram left of the wordmark for horizontal applications
3. Monogram alone for avatars, stamps, favicons, and small placements

## 3. Logo construction and geometry

The mark should be constructed as a modular system rather than drawn loosely.

### Construction principles

- Begin with a square or rectangular modular unit.
- Establish a consistent base unit, `u`, for primary dimensions.
- Build major letter strokes from multiples of `u`.
- Align terminals and intersections to shared axes.
- Use negative space as an active structural element.
- Balance the visual mass optically, not only mathematically.

### Geometric character

The geometry should be sharp, clean, and slightly custom. Avoid soft corners, casual rounding, ornamental flourishes, and generic monogram solutions. Diagonals may be used to create tension or movement, but they should resolve into a stable architectural composition.

The mark should remain recognizable in one color. Gradients, textures, and effects must never be necessary for recognition.

## 4. Clear space and minimum size

### Clear space

Protect the logo with a minimum clear space equal to the height of the monogram’s primary vertical stroke, referred to as `x`.

- Keep all text, borders, imagery, and interface elements outside the `x` zone.
- For high-impact editorial layouts, use `2x` or more.
- Do not allow construction guides to visually collide with the protected logo area unless the guides are intentionally part of a controlled presentation graphic.

### Minimum size

Use the monogram at a size where internal counters and negative spaces remain open and legible.

- Digital: do not reduce below approximately 24 px in height.
- Print: do not reduce below approximately 8 mm in height.
- At smaller sizes, simplify only through an approved small-size variant; never redraw the mark ad hoc.

## 5. Grid and architectural guidelines

The identity may use technical guide lines, measurement ticks, construction grids, crop marks, and alignment axes as supporting devices. These elements should suggest design intelligence and process without making the composition feel unfinished.

### Grid behavior

- Use a restrained modular grid based on the logo’s construction logic.
- Favor generous margins and asymmetrical placements with strong alignment.
- Keep guide lines thin, quiet, and lower in contrast than the logo.
- Allow some lines to extend beyond the main composition as if documenting a plan or elevation.
- Use labels, coordinates, or measurements sparingly and consistently.
- Never let the grid compete with the monogram or headline.

### Recommended line hierarchy

1. Primary logo and headline: highest contrast
2. Main layout rules and axes: medium contrast
3. Construction grid and annotation: low contrast
4. Background surfaces and tonal variation: subtle contrast

## 6. Color palette

The palette is intentionally dark, cool, and architectural, with a restrained violet undertone. Black provides the dominant field; white defines the identity; graphite and deep purple create depth; muted silver supports technical detail.

| Name | HEX | RGB | Primary use |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Void Black | `#000000` | `0, 0, 0` | Primary background, maximum contrast |
| Deep Charcoal | `#0D0C12` | `13, 12, 18` | Secondary background and dark surfaces |
| Graphite | `#1E1C28` | `30, 28, 40` | Panels, cards, tonal separation |
| Soft Black | `#060608` | `6, 6, 8` | Background variation and depth |
| Deep Violet | `#181522` | `24, 21, 34` | Purple-black surfaces and atmospheric depth |
| Architectural Purple | `#29233D` | `41, 35, 61` | Subtle light trails, gradients, and campaign accents |
| Cool Silver | `#B0AEB6` | `176, 174, 182` | Guide lines, metadata, restrained accents |
| Pure White | `#FFFFFF` | `255, 255, 255` | Monogram, primary type, key highlights |

### Suggested ratio

- 70% Void Black
- 15% Deep Charcoal
- 8% Graphite
- 4% Deep Violet / Architectural Purple
- 4% Cool Silver
- 2% Pure White, used selectively for maximum emphasis

### Color rules

- Prefer flat fills over gradients.
- Use Pure White with intention; it should retain visual authority.
- Keep Cool Silver muted and never allow it to become a bright metallic effect.
- Purple may be used as a restrained atmospheric accent, especially in light trails, reflections, tonal gradients, or technical overlays.
- Avoid saturated neon purple or unrelated colors that compete with the identity.
- Check all applications in grayscale to ensure hierarchy remains intact.

## 7. Typography

Typography should combine geometric discipline with refined editorial restraint.

### Wordmark

Use a custom or carefully modified uppercase wordmark with generous tracking. The wordmark should feel precise, calm, and premium rather than loud or corporate.

### Display typography

Use a contemporary grotesk, geometric sans, or restrained neo-grotesk for headlines. Favor:

- Uppercase or controlled title case
- Tight but readable headline spacing
- Strong contrast between large type and open space
- Short, declarative lines

### Supporting typography

Use a neutral sans-serif for captions, navigation, metadata, specifications, and annotations. Small text should be crisp and highly legible.

### Recommended fonts

The following fonts are practical choices for the IVO Studio system. They are widely available, professional, and suitable for digital and print work.

| Role | Primary choice | Alternatives | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display headlines | Space Grotesk | Sora, Plus Jakarta Sans | Large statements, campaign headlines, major titles |
| Studio wordmark support | Manrope | Inter, Helvetica Neue | “IVO STUDIO” lockups and formal supporting text |
| Body copy | Inter | DM Sans, IBM Plex Sans | Descriptions, case studies, website copy |
| Technical metadata | IBM Plex Mono | DM Mono, Space Mono | Coordinates, specifications, labels, measurements |
| Editorial accent | Cormorant Garamond | Instrument Serif, Libre Baskerville | Occasional project titles or refined editorial emphasis |

### Suggested font combinations

**Recommended digital system:** Space Grotesk + Inter + IBM Plex Mono  
This is the most balanced option for a modern, technical, and highly usable identity.

**More editorial system:** Manrope + Instrument Serif + IBM Plex Mono  
Use this when the studio needs a more refined publication or portfolio character.

**Single-family system:** Manrope with IBM Plex Mono for metadata  
Use this for a clean, consistent, and easier-to-manage brand system.

### Font rules

- Use one primary sans-serif family consistently across a project.
- Reserve the mono font for genuine technical information, not decoration.
- Use serif accents sparingly; they should add editorial contrast, not soften the identity.
- Prefer medium, semibold, and bold weights for display type; avoid excessive weight mixing.
- Keep body text at a comfortable reading size with generous line spacing.
- Use uppercase and letter spacing for labels, but never at the expense of legibility.
- Do not use condensed display fonts, novelty futuristic fonts, or overly rounded geometric fonts.

### Logo note

The IVO monogram should remain custom-drawn and should not be recreated by typing letters in a font. Fonts support the identity; they do not replace the logo geometry.

### Typographic rules

- Keep type hierarchy simple: display, supporting, metadata.
- Use tracking as a structural tool, especially for labels and the wordmark.
- Avoid decorative scripts, bubbly display faces, and overly futuristic fonts.
- Do not use more than two type families in one composition.
- Let line breaks feel intentional and architectural.

## 8. Layout system

IVO Studio layouts should feel like considered compositions within a field of space.

### Composition

- Start with a strong margin system.
- Anchor the logo to a clear axis or grid intersection.
- Use asymmetry when it creates balance, not visual noise.
- Give the monogram room to operate as an object.
- Let one dominant element lead each composition.
- Use cropping, scale, and offset to create quiet tension.

### Spacing

Spacing should be generous and consistent. Avoid filling empty areas simply because they are available. Negative space is part of the identity and should be treated as a designed material.

## 9. Writing and verbal identity

IVO Studio’s writing should feel like the visual system: precise, composed, intelligent, and intentionally reduced. The voice should communicate confidence through clarity rather than volume.

### Voice qualities

- **Precise:** Say exactly what is meant; remove filler.
- **Calm:** Use measured language rather than hype or urgency.
- **Intelligent:** Explain the thinking behind the work without over-academizing it.
- **Architectural:** Favor language of structure, space, material, proportion, rhythm, and purpose.
- **Editorial:** Make every sentence feel considered and well composed.
- **Human:** Remain warm and accessible; do not sound cold, corporate, or machine-generated.

### Writing principles

- Lead with the idea, decision, or point of view.
- Prefer short, declarative sentences mixed with occasional thoughtful detail.
- Use active voice and concrete nouns.
- Replace vague claims with observable qualities or specific outcomes.
- Give technical language a clear human meaning.
- Let restraint create authority; do not over-explain.
- Use punctuation sparingly and deliberately.

### Preferred vocabulary

Use words such as:

`structure` · `space` · `form` · `proportion` · `rhythm` · `material` · `clarity` · `system` · `detail` · `tension` · `balance` · `signal` · `craft` · `direction` · `context`

### Avoid

Avoid inflated marketing language, empty superlatives, and trend-heavy phrasing such as “next-level,” “game-changing,” “world-class,” “seamless,” “disruptive,” or “unlock your potential.” Avoid excessive exclamation marks, forced cleverness, and jargon that does not help the reader understand the work.

### Tone by context

| Context | Writing approach |
|---|---|
| Studio introduction | Clear, assured, concise, and point-of-view driven |
| Project description | Describe the problem, the design response, and the resulting experience |
| Case study | Structured, thoughtful, specific, and evidence-led |
| Social caption | Brief, observant, human, and visually aware |
| Technical note | Exact, useful, and free from unnecessary flourish |
| Client communication | Warm, direct, organized, and respectful of the reader’s time |

### Sentence examples

**Preferred:** “We build identities with structure, space, and a clear point of view.”  
**Avoid:** “We create game-changing brand experiences that unlock limitless potential.”

**Preferred:** “The mark is built from a compact geometric system. Its open counters keep the form legible at scale.”  
**Avoid:** “This innovative logo is a bold symbol of our groundbreaking creative vision.”

### Formatting style

- Use sentence case for most body copy.
- Use uppercase selectively for labels, navigation, metadata, and the wordmark.
- Keep headings short and specific.
- Use line breaks to create rhythm in social and editorial writing.
- Use em dashes sparingly; prefer periods, commas, or line breaks when they create cleaner rhythm.
- Keep calls to action direct and understated.

## 10. Imagery and effects

Imagery should support the studio’s architectural and experimental character.

### Preferred imagery

- Abstract architectural details
- Material studies: concrete, metal, glass, stone, paper
- High-contrast shadows and planes
- Technical drawings, elevations, diagrams, and cropped structures
- Minimal editorial still life
- Monochrome or desaturated environments with cool violet undertones

### Effects

Use effects sparingly and with a functional purpose:

- Fine grain for physicality
- Subtle blur or depth separation
- Controlled light falloff
- Thin technical overlays
- Soft tonal shifts between black surfaces

Avoid glossy 3D chrome, neon glow, excessive lens effects, ornamental textures, and visual noise that reduces the logo’s authority.

## 11. Usage rules

### Always

- Preserve the logo’s proportions and geometry.
- Maintain clear space.
- Use approved colors or a one-color treatment.
- Keep the monogram crisp and high contrast.
- Align supporting elements to the established grid.
- Check legibility at the final output size.

### Never

- Stretch, compress, rotate, or skew the logo.
- Add arbitrary outlines, shadows, bevels, or glow.
- Place the mark over a busy image without a controlled field or contrast treatment.
- Recreate the monogram with an unapproved font.
- Crowd the logo with text or decorative elements.
- Introduce bright colors that compete with the monochrome system.
- Treat construction lines as random decoration.

## 12. Social and digital applications

### Profile and avatar

Use the monogram alone, centered or optically balanced within a dark square field. Preserve generous internal breathing room and test the mark at small display sizes.

### Social posts

Use one strong idea per frame. Favor large logo moments, restrained typography, architectural crops, and a clear hierarchy. Construction lines may frame or annotate the composition but should remain secondary.

### Website and interface

Use dark surfaces, crisp type, subtle borders, and spacious sections. Interactions may use Cool Silver or Graphite shifts, but animation should feel deliberate and quiet.

## 13. Print applications

The identity is suited to business cards, stationery, lookbooks, posters, packaging, signage, and studio documentation.

Recommended print treatments include:

- White ink on black stock
- Black ink on warm or cool white stock
- Blind emboss or deboss for understated applications
- Spot silver used sparingly for technical or premium details
- Large-format posters with oversized monogram cropping

Always confirm that fine guide lines and small metadata survive the selected print process.

## 15. Quality checklist

Before approving an asset, confirm:

- The monogram is immediately recognizable.
- The geometry is consistent and optically balanced.
- Clear space is preserved.
- The strongest contrast belongs to the logo or primary message.
- Guide lines support the composition rather than compete with it.
- The palette remains monochrome and cool.
- Typography is legible, restrained, and properly spaced.
- The design still works when reduced, printed, or viewed in grayscale.
- No effect has been added without a clear brand purpose.
